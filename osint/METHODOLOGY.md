
# OSINT Investigation Methodology

## Mission

Lawful public-source identity resolution, historical investigation,
digital-footprint analysis and evidence preservation.

## Core Workflow

RAW LEAD
→ NORMALISE
→ IDENTITY RESOLUTION
→ SOURCE DISCOVERY
→ CROSS-REFERENCE
→ INDEPENDENT CORROBORATION
→ TIMELINE ANALYSIS
→ CONTRADICTION CHECK
→ CONFIDENCE ASSESSMENT
→ EVIDENCE PRESERVATION
→ REPORT

## Identity & Attribution

Investigate and correlate:

- Full names
- Name variants
- Aliases
- Usernames
- Public email addresses
- Employment history
- Professional history
- Business associations
- ABN/ACN/company records
- Public directorship information
- Publicly documented historical locations
- Known public associates
- Domains
- Infrastructure
- Timeline consistency

A matching name alone is never sufficient for attribution.

## Legal & Historical Research

Use:

- Public court material
- Tribunal decisions
- Published judgments
- Regulatory decisions
- Public filings
- Government publications
- Newspaper archives
- Historical web archives
- Legitimate archival collections
- Company and business records

## Digital Footprint

Research may include:

- Public social profiles
- Professional profiles
- GitHub/code presence
- Public usernames
- Domains
- Historical domains
- RDAP/WHOIS
- DNS
- MX records
- Certificate transparency
- Public business infrastructure
- Public documents
- Document metadata

## Correlation Method

For every lead:

1. Normalise the identity data.
2. Search independent sources.
3. Compare identity attributes.
4. Check chronology.
5. Check geography.
6. Check employment/business consistency.
7. Detect common-name collisions.
8. Detect username collisions.
9. Identify contradictory evidence.
10. Distinguish original reporting from copied material.
11. Grade the source.
12. Assign confidence.
13. Preserve significant evidence.
14. Add verified events to the timeline.
15. Pivot from newly verified entities and repeat.

## Source Grades

### A — Primary / Official

Government records, courts, regulators, company registers and official
organisational records.

### B — Strong Secondary

Established journalism, professional publications and reputable databases.

### C — Public Profile

Public social profiles, professional profiles, forums and biographies.

### D — Aggregator

Search indexes, mirrors, people-search indexes and third-party compilations.

### E — Unverified Lead

Information requiring independent corroboration.

## Confidence

HIGH — multiple independent reliable sources and identity attributes agree.

MEDIUM — substantial support exists but verification remains incomplete.

LOW — plausible lead with insufficient corroboration.

REJECTED — evidence indicates another person/entity or attribution fails.

## Timeline

Use:

DATE
→ EVENT
→ PERSON/ORGANISATION
→ LOCATION
→ SOURCE
→ CONFIDENCE

Locations must come from legitimate historical/public evidence.

Do not infer covert or real-time whereabouts.

## Evidence Preservation

For important findings record:

- Evidence ID
- URL
- Source title
- Publisher/platform
- Publication date
- Collection date/time
- Claim supported
- Identity attributes matched
- Corroborating source
- Contradictions
- Source grade
- Confidence
- Evidence filename
- SHA-256 hash

## Reporting

Always separate:

FACT

INFERENCE

UNVERIFIED LEAD

Never report an inference as established fact.

## Operating Boundary

Public and passive collection is the default.

Do not obtain private account access, credentials, hidden telecom
records, restricted databases or non-public communications.

Active application or infrastructure testing requires explicit
authorisation.
